The Diffusion group at MIT's Computer Science and Artificial Intelligence Laboratory (CSAIL) focuses on advancing generative artificial intelligence, particularly diffusion models. These models generate high-quality images, audio, and other data types by gradually denoising random noise into structured outputs.

Key research areas include score-based generative modeling, latent diffusion, and conditional generation. The group develops algorithms that reduce the computational cost of sampling while maintaining output fidelity. They explore how diffusion models can be integrated with other architectures like transformers and GANs to enhance performance.

Applications span medical imaging, scientific simulation, content creation, and data augmentation. "We aim to build models that not only generate realistic data but also allow precise user control." Their findings are regularly published in top-tier machine learning conferences including NeurIPS, ICML, and CVPR.
